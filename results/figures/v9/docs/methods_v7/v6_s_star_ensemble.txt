====================================================================================================
v6 CRITICAL RESERVATION SHARE s*, ACROSS THE FULL 20-MEMBER HYDROLOGY ENSEMBLE
====================================================================================================

  s* = 1 - demand / (dry-season runoff x extractable fraction)

  s* is the non-power reservation share at which a basin's coal water demand exactly exhausts
  the environmental-flow allowance. A basin breaches at China's measured reservation s = 0.85
  whenever s* < 0.85. A POSITIVE s* means the basin does NOT breach when power is given the
  ENTIRE extractable dry-season flow -- the overshoot is then a question of ALLOCATION.
  A NEGATIVE s* means the basin breaches even at zero reservation -- genuine SCARCITY.

  WHY THIS SUPERSEDES THE v5 STATEMENT. v5 computed s* on the single SOLVED member
  (cwatm|gfdl-esm4|ssp126) and reported that it is positive in every basin and every year, with
  and without capture, concluding that the constraint is allocation throughout. That is correct
  for that member and correct for the unabated fleet on ALL members -- but it is NOT correct for
  full capture across the ensemble, and the exception is the one basin the paper is about.

  THE RESULT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  UNABATED: s* > 0 in 20 of 20 members, every basin, every planning year, minimum +0.127.
    Today's coal fleet fits inside a 20% environmental-flow allowance in every hydrological
    future examined, IF power is given the whole extractable share. The present-day overshoot
    -- three basins, 765 GW, 54% of the standing fleet at s = 0.85 -- is therefore entirely a
    statement about who holds the water, not about how much there is.

  FULL CAPTURE: the Hai flips, and only the Hai.
    Negative in 13 of 20 members at 2030 and 12 of 20 at 2040. Under WaterGAP2 9 of 10 members
    are negative at 2030; under CWatM 4 of 10. Both SSPs give negatives, so this is not one
    hydrology model and not one emissions scenario.

  So capture does not merely tighten an allocation constraint -- IN ONE BASIN IT CONVERTS AN
  ALLOCATION PROBLEM INTO A SCARCITY PROBLEM. Everywhere else, and for the fleet as a whole,
  the constraint remains allocation at every reservation share.
